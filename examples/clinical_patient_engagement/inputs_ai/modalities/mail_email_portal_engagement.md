## Summary

Mail, email, and portals are slower than SMS or calls but can support durable instructions, accessibility, caregiver review, and formal notices. They are especially useful as fallback or reinforcement channels.

## Key Findings

- Medicaid and HCBS populations may require non-digital or caregiver-supported channels because phone and broadband access varies.
- Portal messages can preserve context but may not reach members who do not regularly use portals.
- Mail can support formal notices, care plan summaries, and durable reminders, but should not be the only urgent channel.
- Email can be useful for caregivers and administrative content when consent and privacy rules allow.

## Practical Tactics or Workflow Patterns

- Use mail for durable information and fallback when digital contact fails.
- Use portal/email for longer explanations and attachments, not urgent time-sensitive engagement alone.
- Track channel reachability and patient/caregiver preferences.

## Transferability Assessment

Cross-industry lifecycle messaging maps to healthcare only when it avoids promotional tone and protects privacy.

## Operational Implications

The platform should support multi-channel history and allow staff to choose lower-tech alternatives.

## Compliance, Privacy, Safety, Trust, and Accessibility Caveats

Do not assume portal access or email privacy for Medicaid, caregiver, or shared-device contexts.

## Limitations

This is an initial internet-informed baseline. It combines public evidence, public guidance, public vendor pages, and strategy synthesis. Vendor claims are not treated as independent proof of effectiveness.
