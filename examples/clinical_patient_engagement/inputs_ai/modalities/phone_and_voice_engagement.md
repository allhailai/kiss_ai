## Summary

Phone and voice outreach are important for complex, sensitive, or accessibility-dependent engagement. AI voice can help with low-risk scripted workflows, but live human support remains important for trust, ambiguity, caregiver consent, and safety.

## Key Findings

- Personalized calls and SMS reminders both improve attendance in some settings, with phone calls useful when a richer interaction is needed.
- AI voice vendors describe healthcare use cases such as scheduling, intake, reminders, triage routing, and answering services.
- The FCC has confirmed that TCPA restrictions on artificial or prerecorded voices apply to AI-generated human voices.
- Voice workflows need clear disclosure, consent review, escalation, and fallback to live humans.

## Practical Tactics or Workflow Patterns

- Reserve live calls for high-risk, low-trust, repeated non-response, caregiver coordination, or confusing situations.
- Use AI voice for low-risk first-touch or administrative check-ins only when consent, disclosure, and fallback rules are clear.
- Capture call outcomes as structured data: reached, wrong number, caregiver involved, callback requested, escalation needed.

## Transferability Assessment

Contact center escalation and warm-transfer patterns transfer well, but healthcare calls need more careful identity, consent, and empathy handling.

## Operational Implications

The platform needs call scripts, AI disclosure rules, live transfer, call summaries, QA sampling, and consent-aware caregiver workflows.

## Compliance, Privacy, Safety, Trust, and Accessibility Caveats

AI voice should not give clinical advice or handle distress without human escalation.

## Limitations

This is an initial internet-informed baseline. It combines public evidence, public guidance, public vendor pages, and strategy synthesis. Vendor claims are not treated as independent proof of effectiveness.
