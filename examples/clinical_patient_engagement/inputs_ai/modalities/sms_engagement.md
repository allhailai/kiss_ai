## Summary

SMS is scalable and effective for simple reminders, confirmations, and low-risk follow-up, but clinical content should be limited, consent and opt-out must be managed, and caregiver messaging needs extra care.

## Key Findings

- Targeted text reminders can reduce missed clinic visits and may be more efficient when focused on patients at higher risk of no-show.
- SMS works best for short, clear administrative or care coordination messages with easy actions such as confirm, reschedule, or request a call.
- HIPAA guidance commonly warns that standard SMS may lack encryption, access controls, and audit controls, so PHI should be minimized unless using a compliant platform and appropriate agreements.
- TCPA-related healthcare exemptions may apply to treatment-related messages, but marketing or promotional content changes the consent posture.

## Practical Tactics or Workflow Patterns

- Use short plain-language messages with one requested action.
- Provide opt-out language and a path to a human.
- Avoid sensitive diagnosis or service details in SMS unless the platform, consent, and policy allow it.

## Transferability Assessment

SMS tactics from retail and subscription engagement transfer only when they remain respectful, consent-aware, and clinically appropriate.

## Operational Implications

AI can draft and classify SMS, but higher-risk replies should route to humans. The platform needs consent status, preferred language, caregiver permissions, and opt-out state.

## Compliance, Privacy, Safety, Trust, and Accessibility Caveats

SMS may be inaccessible for some members and may expose sensitive information if phones are shared.

## Limitations

This is an initial internet-informed baseline. It combines public evidence, public guidance, public vendor pages, and strategy synthesis. Vendor claims are not treated as independent proof of effectiveness.
