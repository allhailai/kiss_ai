## Summary

Strong healthcare engagement programs combine modality choice, patient segmentation, care coordination, stakeholder feedback, and clear escalation into live support. For Medicaid MLTSS and HCBS populations, engagement should account for caregivers, home-based services, accessibility, and trust.

## Key Findings

- CMS describes MLTSS as Medicaid managed care for long-term services and supports, often covering home and community-based services and care coordination.
- NASHP highlights integrated care coordination, stakeholder engagement, advisory structures, and closing the loop with members as important MLTSS practices.
- Appointment reminder research supports targeted reminders and shows SMS can reduce missed visits, while personalized phone calls may be useful for selected populations.
- Healthcare examples should be separated from vendor claims because many vendor case studies report outcomes without independent validation.

## Practical Tactics or Workflow Patterns

- Start with Medicaid and dual eligible members who have high outreach need or risk of service disruption.
- Use caregiver/proxy workflows when consent and relationship context allow.
- Close the loop by recording what happened after each outreach attempt and whether a human follow-up is needed.

## Transferability Assessment

Directly relevant because the target population includes Medicaid, dual eligible, MLTSS, and HCBS waiver populations.

## Operational Implications

Baseline outputs should favor general strategy and operational playbooks before detailed scripts. Human review is important for consent, caregiver outreach, and high-risk cases.

## Compliance, Privacy, Safety, Trust, and Accessibility Caveats

Do not assume one engagement channel fits all Medicaid members. Digital access, caregiver involvement, language, disability, and trust vary widely.

## Limitations

This is an initial internet-informed baseline. It combines public evidence, public guidance, public vendor pages, and strategy synthesis. Vendor claims are not treated as independent proof of effectiveness.
