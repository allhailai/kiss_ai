## Summary

Engagement strategies should be measured with operational, patient experience, safety, and equity metrics. Experiments should start with low-risk workflows and include monitoring for unintended consequences.

## Key Findings

- No-show reduction, reachability, confirmation, cancellation/rescheduling, completion, and care gap closure are practical healthcare metrics.
- Cross-industry engagement programs use lifecycle metrics, channel attribution, segmentation, A/B testing, and churn or disengagement signals.
- Healthcare measurement should include opt-outs, complaints, accessibility failures, inappropriate automation, and escalation quality.
- AI workflows need QA sampling, transcript review, error tracking, and human override metrics.

## Practical Tactics or Workflow Patterns

- Measure reach, response, completion, escalation, human workload, and patient trust signals.
- Use pilots before scaling AI-heavy workflows.
- Report results by segment to detect inequity or access issues.

## Transferability Assessment

A/B testing and lifecycle analytics transfer from consumer engagement if clinical risk and equity are monitored.

## Operational Implications

Outputs should include measurement plans and monitoring requirements for each strategy family.

## Compliance, Privacy, Safety, Trust, and Accessibility Caveats

Optimizing only for response rates can damage trust or increase burden.

## Limitations

This is an initial internet-informed baseline. It combines public evidence, public guidance, public vendor pages, and strategy synthesis. Vendor claims are not treated as independent proof of effectiveness.
