## Summary

The priority population includes Medicaid-only and dual eligible members, likely including ABD and MLTSS/HCBS waiver members. Engagement design should account for caregivers, disability, language, health literacy, digital access, and historical trust barriers.

## Key Findings

- MLTSS programs serve people needing long-term services and supports, often emphasizing home and community-based services and care coordination.
- Stakeholder engagement and person-centered planning are central in MLTSS improvement work.
- Digital-only engagement can miss members with limited digital access, shared devices, disability, low literacy, or caregiver dependence.
- Personalization should be used to reduce burden and increase relevance, not to manipulate or pressure members.

## Practical Tactics or Workflow Patterns

- Segment by reachability, care complexity, caregiver involvement, language, disability accommodation, digital access, and trust level.
- Use human-led outreach for low-trust, complex, or sensitive cases.
- Offer channel choice and clear human fallback.

## Transferability Assessment

Customer segmentation transfers only when it is transparent, respectful, and clinically appropriate.

## Operational Implications

The platform should support caregiver/proxy fields, language preference, consent status, accessibility notes, and channel preference.

## Compliance, Privacy, Safety, Trust, and Accessibility Caveats

Avoid stigmatizing segments or using protected attributes in ways that create inequity.

## Limitations

This is an initial internet-informed baseline. It combines public evidence, public guidance, public vendor pages, and strategy synthesis. Vendor claims are not treated as independent proof of effectiveness.
