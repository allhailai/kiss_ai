# Goal Requirements

## Project Objective

Research, discover, and synthesize best-in-class patient engagement strategies for virtual and telehealth-based clinical service delivery companies. The project should identify leading healthcare examples and borrow transferable engagement practices from adjacent industries such as consumer technology, financial services, retail, hospitality, education, and subscription services.

The project should turn those examples into practical workflows, modality choices, useful tips, and repeatable playbooks that help clinical teams engage patients, improve follow-through, and maintain trust across SMS, phone calls, mail, email, patient portals, and other outreach channels.

This project should assume we are building a fully customizable agentic engagement platform for clinical service companies. The platform may integrate with telephony and messaging vendors such as Twilio or similar SMS/VOIP providers, and voice-generation or voice-agent vendors such as ElevenLabs or similar tools. It may use generative AI for SMS, voice calls, workflow orchestration, and data curation, while still requiring human oversight, clinical safety, privacy, and trust-preserving design.

## Audience

- Clinical operations leaders
- Patient engagement teams
- Care coordination and navigation leaders
- Product and workflow designers supporting virtual care delivery
- LLM agents maintaining the engagement knowledge base and outputs

## In Scope

- Patient engagement strategy for virtual and telehealth-based clinical services.
- Best-in-class examples from healthcare companies, care delivery organizations, digital health companies, and patient engagement vendors.
- Transferable engagement patterns from non-healthcare industries, clearly labeled as adapted practices rather than healthcare evidence.
- Outreach workflows across SMS, phone, mailing, email, patient portals, and hybrid sequences.
- Practical tips, tactics, nudges, copy patterns, timing rules, cadence ideas, and escalation patterns.
- Agentic patient interaction strategies using configurable AI agents across SMS, voice calls, phone trees, portals, and human handoff workflows.
- Agentic data-curation strategies that help gather, normalize, summarize, classify, and maintain engagement evidence, patient context, workflow outcomes, and open questions.
- Platform design degrees of freedom, including custom workflows, custom user interfaces, configurable scripts/prompts, vendor integrations, escalation rules, and analytics dashboards.
- Patient segmentation, consent, cadence, escalation, and closed-loop follow-up workflows.
- Operational playbooks for care teams, schedulers, navigators, and clinical support staff.
- Evidence and best practices from healthcare engagement, behavioral science, and care management.

## Out of Scope

- Final legal advice.
- Domain-specific operational workflows unless later added by requirements.
- Marketing-only campaigns disconnected from clinical care delivery.
- Engineering implementation details for a specific messaging platform unless later added.
- Blindly copying non-healthcare tactics that could undermine clinical trust, accessibility, consent, privacy, or patient safety.
- Fully autonomous clinical decision-making without human review or appropriate clinical governance.

## Decision Criteria

Outputs should help determine:

- which engagement modalities fit different patient populations and clinical goals;
- which best-in-class strategies are proven in healthcare versus adapted from other industries;
- which tips and tactics are practical enough for clinical operations teams to use;
- where agentic automation is appropriate versus where human clinical staff should remain in the loop;
- which vendor capabilities, such as SMS, VOIP, voice generation, conversational agents, or workflow automation, are strategically useful;
- what data-curation workflows agents can safely perform to keep engagement knowledge and patient context current;
- what custom UI or workflow features would materially improve patient engagement operations;
- what outreach sequence should happen before, during, and after a virtual clinical encounter;
- when to escalate from asynchronous outreach to live calls or mailed communication;
- how to preserve patient trust, accessibility, consent, and privacy;
- which open questions require human operational, compliance, or clinical review.

## Constraints

- Do not invent evidence.
- Cite source support for material claims.
- Clearly distinguish healthcare evidence, industry examples, expert opinion, and adapted non-healthcare tactics.
- Evaluate transferability: explain why a tactic from another industry should or should not apply to clinical service delivery.
- Distinguish agentic automation, clinician-assisted automation, and human-only workflows.
- Treat generative AI outputs as drafts or agent actions requiring governance, monitoring, and escalation rules.
- Consider vendor capabilities and constraints without locking recommendations to one vendor unless the requirements later name a preferred stack.
- Separate evidence-backed practices from hypotheses or open questions.
- Keep recommendations operational and usable by non-technical clinical teams.
